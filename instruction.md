The compliance return for bore PB-07 is due. The licence wants the water level for every day of the abstraction period, the depth to water on the day the licence names, the drawdown rate, and whether the bore is inside its licence. The export and the licence settings are in `/app/data`, and its README says what each file holds, what the readings carry, and how the last return was put together. The hydro team's tools are Java: `/app/bore` has a stub at `/app/bore/src/Drawdown.java` and a build script, `/app/bore/build.sh`, which is there if you want it; we don't run it.

- `java -cp /app/bore/out Drawdown <data-dir> <output-json>` writes one JSON object with exactly five keys: `daily_depth_m`, an object keyed `YYYY-MM-DD` with one entry per calendar day from the date of `abstraction_start` to the calendar date of the last reading in `levels.csv` inclusive (the last day is keyed even when its 24 hours run past the last reading), giving the mean depth to water over that day; `projected_depth_m`, the mean depth over the day of abstraction that `projection_day` names; `drawdown_mm_per_day`; `tidal_period_h`, the period you read off that bore's `commissioning_10min.csv`, in hours; and `within_licence`, true or false
- a day is the 24 hours that begin at the time of day the abstraction started, so the day keyed 2025-08-04 runs from `abstraction_start` on that date. The drawdown rate is the fall from the mean on the day abstraction started to the projected depth, in mm, divided by `projection_day`. `within_licence` is true when that rate is inside `licence_limit_mm_per_day` and the projected depth stays above `pump_intake_depth_m`, both from the `bore.json` you were given
- the readings carry a tidal sinusoid at the period the bore breathes at, between 10 and 15 hours and different on every bore, evaluated at each reading's own time, on top of the level the licence is about, which is flat before `abstraction_start` and falls at a steady rate after it for as long as the abstraction runs; the projected depth is that fall carried out to `projection_day`. How you fit the tidal term and the rate of fall is your call, including which readings you use
- the licence, `pump_intake_depth_m` and the regulator's record are depth below the casing top. On every record the measuring point moved off the casing top once, at a stamp between two readings: `bore.json` gives the stamp as `measuring_point_moved_at` and the height of the point above the casing top from then on as `measuring_point_offset_m`, and every reading in `levels.csv` from that stamp on is that much deeper than the depth below the casing top. The export is not corrected for it
- the box is offline and the standard library that ships with Java is all you have. We compile `src/*.java` with javac ourselves and run the class from `/app/bore` with nothing on the box executable but java while it runs. Before we compile, anything under `src` that is not plain text is dropped, and the run itself cannot start another process or load a native library, so the standard library is genuinely all there is to reach for. The same class is run unchanged on the exports from other bores, the same files in the same format with their own dates, settings and measuring point move, so nothing about this bore goes into the source. Every compliance record is logged every twelve or twenty four hours, never a whole multiple of the period. The files in `/app/data` stay as shipped

We check against the licence check as it was done: the contractor's 10 minute logger went back on the bore through the abstraction period and on the licence day, and the regulator takes each day's mean over those 24 hours, tide included, since a day isn't a whole number of tidal cycles. On every bore: each day's level within 0.03 m of that day's mean, the projected depth within 0.10 m of the licence day's mean, the rate within 0.13 m spread over `projection_day` days of the check's, the period within 0.15 h of the one the dump carries, and the verdict the same as the check's. No bore in this survey sits within those tolerances of either limit, so the verdict is never decided at the boundary.
